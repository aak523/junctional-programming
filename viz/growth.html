GPU World / Capacity formation

How a kH of compute comes into existence, and how long it takes. The growth model behind the globe — ordering, queueing, energising, serving.

01

The identity

Capacity is not a stock you buy — it is an intersection. Compute counts in a month only where silicon, power and a site coincide. Everything else in the model is a different way of paying to make that intersection happen sooner.

capacity(site, month) = Σgen powered(gen) × compute(gen) + Σ active reservations + min(spot request, spot cap)
where powered(N) = min( owned(N), MW ÷ 1.6 ) and powered(H) = min( owned(H), (MW − powered(N)×1.6) ÷ 1.4 )

Owned, powered

Delivered GPUs at the site, capped by live megawatts. Gen N is powered first; gen H takes what is left. 1 kH of H needs 1.4 MW, of N 1.6 MW — but N is worth 2 kH per thousand units.

Reserved

Cloud contracts active this month. No power of yours, no lead time past one month — and no way out: the whole term is paid, including months past the horizon.

Spot

A standing request, filled at month end up to the site's supply. Instant, uncapped in price, and the first thing to vanish when the regime turns tight.

Megawatts

Not a term in the sum — a ceiling on the first one. Signed months in advance, live for 24 months, paid whether or not a GPU sits under it.

03

The pipeline

The same picture on one axis: months from the decision to the first kH-month served. Silicon and power are two independent clocks, and compute appears only where both have finished — which is why a fleet can sit delivered and dark for a quarter.
